Transcribe the words as performed. This page covers the words themselves; [Spacing](./spacing.mdx) and [Punctuation](./punctuation.mdx) cover how the line is marked up.

## Grammar and spelling

Keep intentional slang, fragments, omitted words, and nonstandard grammar. Do not add missing verbs, remove double negatives, or replace unusual phrasing just to make a lyric read like formal prose.

For example, this line should retain its wordplay:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1752214923"
  quote={"That's that me espresso"}
/>

Correct spelling mistakes that do not belong to the performance. For words that sound alike, use the meaning of the phrase to choose the spelling.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"You're only happy when your sorry head is filled with dope"}
  incorrect={"Your only happy when you're sorry head is filled with dope"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1440827631" }}
/>

When a word is unclear, replay the recording and consult an artist- or publisher-provided lyric source. Do not substitute a word solely because it produces a more grammatical sentence.

## Other languages and accents

Preserve accents and other marks in names and words. Keep passages in other languages in their original language and follow that language's writing rules.

## Contractions and apostrophes

### Contractions

Keep contractions when they are sung. Use an apostrophe where letters are omitted, and keep it attached to the word.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"I know it'll kill me when it's over"}
  incorrect={"I know itll kill me when it 's over"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1170698158" }}
/>

### Shortened words

Use established spellings for shortened words. A missing initial letter does not remove the need to capitalize a line's first word.

```text
'Cause
'Bout
'Em
Lovin'
```

Here `'Cause` keeps its apostrophe and is still capitalized as the first word of the line:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1031416179"
  quote={"'Cause you know, I just wanna see you smile"}
/>

A dropped final letter takes the apostrophe at the end instead:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1031416179"
  quote={"Now don't you worry, it won't be long, darlin'"}
/>

The same applies to a word that is only partly sung, even when it is not an established short form. Write the part performed and mark the missing opening with an apostrophe. Do not restore the rest of the word because another line, another voice, or the written phrase contains it in full.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1523763061"
  quote={`I make it known I fuck with you, not undercover
('Cover)`}
/>

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1734167854"
  quote={`A breakthrough that can bring us anywhere
('Nywhere)`}
/>

The full word is sung once. The echo picks up only its final syllables, so it is written `'Cover`, not `Undercover`.

### Forms written without an apostrophe

Some informal forms are written without apostrophes. Do not add one simply because a word is informal, or expand the word into a different sung phrase.

```text
Gonna
Wanna
Gotta
Tryna
```

Both forms stay as sung, with no apostrophe added:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1170698158"
  quote={"I don't wanna think about it, I just wanna love you now, oh yeah"}
/>

### Plurals and possessives

Distinguish an ordinary plural from a possessive. The apostrophe indicates ownership, not just the presence of more than one person.

**Plural**

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="91989806"
  quote={"The lights are on, but there's no one home"}
/>

**Possessive**

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1657869393"
  quote={"You was at the farmers' market with your perfect peach"}
/>

The second line is a plural possessive, so its apostrophe follows the `s`. It also keeps the nonstandard `You was`, which belongs to the performance.

### Apostrophe style

Use a consistent apostrophe style throughout the song. Do not mix straight and curly apostrophes or use a backtick in their place.

## Capitalization

Begin every lyric line with a capital letter, including a line that continues the previous sentence. Use normal capitalization for the rest of the line.

<CodeComparison
  correct={`We don't talk anymore
Like we used to do`}
  incorrect={`We don't talk anymore
like we used to do`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1031416185" }}
/>

The second line cannot stand alone as a sentence, but it still begins with a capital letter.

Capitalize:

- The first-person pronoun.
- Proper names, brands, and places.
- Acronyms.
- Names for deities, gods, and goddesses, along with pronouns that refer to them.
- Titles of sacred texts and named religious events.

Preserve the established capitalization of names. Do not capitalize every word or use all capitals to indicate loud singing.

<CodeComparison
  correct="I got a California king"
  incorrect={`I Got A California King
I GOT A CALIFORNIA KING`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1698723208" }}
/>

Do not copy a track title's lowercase or uppercase styling into the lyric text. Capitalize a quoted title according to the title itself.

A lyric styled in a single case from beginning to end is a separate case. See [Expressive lyrics](./expressive.mdx).

Do not extend divine capitalization to ordinary references to another person.

## Repeated lyrics

Write every repeated word and line in full. Do not replace a chorus with a repetition instruction or assume that later choruses have identical words.

<CodeComparison
  correct={`Please, please, please (Please)
Please, please, please (Please)
Please, please, please (Please, please)`}
  incorrect="Please, please, please (Repeat x3)"
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1750307080" }}
/>

The three lines above carry different backing vocals, so a repetition instruction would lose the last one.

Use commas for separate repetitions of complete words within a phrase. Use hyphens for repeated fragments that belong to one word. Both appear in this line, first the complete word, then the fragment:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="529843559"
  quote={"Tonight we're going hard, hard, h-h-h-hard"}
/>

## Background vocals

### Line-synced lyrics

For line-based synchronization only, put a short backing phrase in parentheses at the end of the associated lead line, following the [Apple Music for Artists convention](https://artists.apple.com/support/1111-lyrics-guidelines). Capitalize its first word and keep any punctuation that belongs to it inside the parentheses.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1440843077"
  quote={"Won't you come and put it down on me? (Down on me)"}
/>

### Word-synced lyrics

Put the backing phrase on its own subline beneath the associated lead line. Keep its words and timing separate from the lead, including where the vocals overlap. Do not append the backing phrase to the lead text in parentheses.

The same passage, with the second line as the backing subline. Its first word begins before the lead line's last word ends, and the timings stay separate:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1440843077"
  quote={`Won't you come and put it down on me?
(Down on me)`}
/>

See [Background vocals in TTML](../../../ttml/background-vocals.mdx) for the file structure and timing of a backing subline.

For either synchronization type, an interjection sung by the lead vocalist stays in the lead text. Do not mark it as a backing vocal just because it is short.

A backing part is a phrase someone sang. A delay tail, a reverb wash, or a chopped vocal texture is the production repeating the lead rather than a second performer, so it gets no subline. See [Leave out incidental sounds](#leave-out-incidental-sounds).

## Non-word vocals

Include recognizable non-word vocals when they form part of the melody, hook, or a meaningful response. Choose the spelling by listening to the vowel, the way the sound begins and ends, and the energy of the delivery.

Judge the sound by ear. The duration of a timed word or syllable does not determine its spelling. A long breathy ending can follow a short vowel, and a connected vocal phrase can contain several different sounds.

### Oh and Ooh

Use Ooh when the vowel itself is drawn out. Use Oh when the vowel is short, even if the following breathy sound lasts longer. Do not change Oh to Ooh simply because its timing interval is long.

The standalone vocalization:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1657869393" quote={"Oh"} />

A sustained vowel:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1560735480" quote={"Ooh"} />

### Connected sounds and audible pauses

Join connected non-word sounds with hyphens. Use a comma when an audible pause separates the sounds. A phrase can contain both connected sounds and separate utterances.

These sounds flow together:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="296016899" quote={"Oh-oh-oh"} />

Elsewhere in the same song, a pause separates the first sound from the connected sounds that follow:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="296016899" quote={"Oh, oh-oh-oh"} />

An opening that mixes pauses and connected sounds:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="576670459"
  quote={`Oh, oh-oh-oh
Oh-oh-oh, oh`}
/>

Keep the first sound as Oh: its vowel is brief, while the breathy ending lasts longer. Listen to the vowel rather than judging from the total length of the sound.

### Ah and Ha

Use Ha when the sound has an audible h at the beginning, and Ah when it begins with the vowel. Preserve the difference when the singer moves between them.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1434371887"
  quote={`Oh, ha, ah, ha, ah
Ha, ah`}
/>

### Woo and Whoo

Both spellings can be appropriate. Choose according to the delivery. Whoo tends to suit a sharper, more energetic vocalization; do not select it solely because the sound lasts longer.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1698723221"
  quote={"Woo, you comin' home with me?"}
/>

A sharper vocalization:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1440843077" quote={"Whoo!"} />

The Woo above is held longer than this Whoo, so length alone does not decide the spelling.

### Whoa and Woah

Prefer Whoa, the conventional spelling. Woah is an acceptable variant, but use Whoa consistently when authoring lyrics.

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1691699836" quote={"Whoa"} />

### Avoid extra letters for duration

Use the established spelling of the vocal sound. Do not add extra letters just to represent a held note, a long breath, or an emphatic delivery. The timing carries the duration.

For a quoted vocalization, use:

<CodeComparison
  correct={'my brain goes, "ah"'}
  incorrect={'my brain goes, "ahhhh"'}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1694386828" }}
/>

### Leave out incidental sounds

Do not transcribe every incidental breath or turn improvised scatting into a long string of invented syllables. Do not insert descriptions of instrumental sounds or sound effects.

Leave out vocals that production has turned into texture rather than words. Chopped, reversed, pitched, or filtered vocals used as an instrumental layer carry no performed word to write, and a delay or reverb tail is the same phrase still ringing rather than a new one. Transcribe what a performer sang, not what a processor did to it afterwards.

Judge this by what is audible, not by how the sound was made. A processed vocal that still delivers intelligible words stays in the text, and a repeat sung as a separate vocal part is a [background vocal](#background-vocals) rather than an effect. A cut-off word is also a performance, so keep it and mark it as described in [Hyphens and interruptions](./punctuation.mdx#hyphens-and-interruptions).

## Spoken passages

Transcribe audible spoken passages and conversational interludes, including introductions, dialogue, and spoken responses. Keep the words as performed, even when they are not sung or rapped.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1894533119"
  quote={"Tell every look, tell every right from the—"}
/>

When the main artist or a featured artist speaks, assign the passage to that performer's vocal part. When someone outside those artists speaks, assign the passage to the **Other** vocal part. Do not attribute another person's dialogue to the artist just because it appears in their track.

Keep speaker labels, section headings, and editorial instructions outside the lyric text. See [Agents and voices](../../../ttml/agents.mdx#spoken-passages) for speaker assignments in the file.

## Clean and explicit lyrics

Match the version of the recording. Transcribe uncensored words in full when they are audible. This includes profanity and slurs, including the N-word. Do not mask a word solely because it is offensive; censor it only when the audio censors it.

When the word is audible and uncensored:

<CodeComparison
  correct={"'Cause you only listen to your fucking friends"}
  incorrect={"'Cause you only listen to your ****ing friends"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1564531202" }}
/>

When the recording bleeps, mutes, or otherwise censors part or all of a word, replace the whole word with asterisks rather than leaving part of it visible:

<CodeComparison
  correct={"Fuckin' with you feel like jail, ****"}
  incorrect={"Fuckin' with you feel like jail, b****"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1574004703" }}
/>

That line censors one word and keeps the other, because only one is censored in the audio.

If the singer performs a replacement word in a clean version, transcribe that word. Do not restore words from the explicit version or add censorship that is absent from the recording.
