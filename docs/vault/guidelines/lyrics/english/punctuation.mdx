---
title: Punctuation
description: Line endings, commas, questions, quotations, and how to mark an interrupted line
---

Use punctuation to clarify meaning without changing the delivery. This page covers the marks themselves; [Spacing](./spacing.mdx) covers the spaces around them.

## Line endings

Do not add a period or comma at the end of a lyric line, even when the sentence is complete. Keep question marks, exclamation points, and closing quotation marks when the meaning requires them.

<CodeComparison
  correct={`When you said you were passing through
Was I even on your way?`}
  incorrect={`When you said you were passing through,
Was I even on your way.`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1564531202" }}
/>

Preserve punctuation that belongs to the spelling of a name or abbreviation. Do not add an extra sentence-ending period after it.

## Commas

Use commas within a line to separate list items, set off a direct address, or clarify a clause. Do not insert a comma at every breath or beat.

**Direct address**

<CodeComparison

## Questions and exclamations

Put a question mark where the question ends. If it continues onto another line, wait until that line to close it. See [Questions across lines](./splits.mdx#questions-across-lines).

A question mark can also appear inside a line when another thought follows it:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1752214923"
  quote={"Is it that sweet? I guess so"}
/>

Do not add a question mark merely because a phrase contains a question word. An indirect question can be part of a statement.

**Direct question**

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1696819855"
  quote={"What was I made for?"}
/>

**Statement containing an indirect question**

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1696819855"
  quote={"What I was made for"}
/>

Those two lines are adjacent in the same song. Only the word order tells them apart, so the question mark has to follow the grammar rather than the vocabulary.

Use an exclamation point for an exclamation. Do not add repeated punctuation to represent volume or emphasis.

## Quotation marks

Use quotation marks for words quoted within the lyric. Do not wrap the entire lyric in quotation marks.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1468058171"
  quote={"Said, \"I'm fine,\" but it wasn't true"}
/>

Keep a quotation open across line breaks until the quoted passage ends. Place a question mark inside the closing quotation mark when the quoted words are the question.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="269573364"
  quote={`I said, "Don't mind, but what do you mean I am the one
Who will dance on the floor in the round?"`}
/>

## Hyphens and interruptions

Use hyphens for ordinary compounds, repeated word fragments, and letters sung individually. Do not add spaces around a word-internal hyphen.

**Compound**

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1440862803"
  quote={"Hope is a four-letter word"}
/>

**Repeated onset**:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1440818663" quote={"We're c-coming out"} />

**Spelled-out words**:

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1698723221" quote={"H-O-T T-O G-O"} />

Keep spaces between separate spelled-out words. Preserve the letters actually performed, even if the spelling is unconventional.

A hyphen inside a stammer such as `c-coming` is not an interruption. The word still finishes, so the hyphens stay inside it.

One mark ends an interrupted phrase: the em dash. Whether the last word is complete does not change the mark, and a hyphen never ends a phrase.

The dash applies only when the performance cuts the phrase off. If the singer carries the line to a natural close, it takes no mark, even when the sentence continues on the next line or never finishes.

### Em dash

Use an em dash when the voice stops abruptly. Attach it to the last sung letter with no space. If the word itself breaks off, write only the part performed and do not complete a word the singer never finishes.

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="1736636955" quote={"I think we got p—"} />

<AppleMusicQuote region="us" songId="269573364" quote={"Billie Jean is—"} />

A trailing hyphen is not an interruption mark, even on a partial word:

<CodeComparison
  correct={"Thunder, thunder, thun—"}
  incorrect={"Thunder, thunder, thun-"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1411629089" }}
/>

When the singer breaks off and restarts the phrase, end each break with an em dash and follow it with a space. Do not add a comma after the dash.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"I don't de— I don't de— I don't deserve you tonight"}
  incorrect={"I don't de-, I don't de-, I don't deserve you tonight"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1556175647" }}
/>

### No mark

Leave the end of a line bare when it is sung to a natural close. An unfinished sentence is not an interruption on its own; the performance has to cut it off. A line that trails away or fades out also ends bare, because the timing already shows the fade.

Do not use ellipses in lyric text, whether for a trailing line, a hesitation, a breath, an instrumental break, or a sentence that continues on the next line. A held note alone does not require extra letters or hyphens.
