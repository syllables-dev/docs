---
title: Expressive lyrics
description: Whole-track case styling, stylized titles, and which to keep
---

Some artists style a whole song or album in a single case instead of using normal capitalization. Keep the styling when it does not slow the reader down. Replace it with normal capitalization when it does.

This page covers styling that runs through an entire track, and stylized titles that surface inside single lines. For capitalization inside an ordinary line, see [Capitalization](./text.mdx#capitalization).

## Lowercase throughout

Keep a lyric written entirely in lowercase. Lowercase letters rise above and drop below the line, so each word keeps a distinct shape and the text reads at normal speed. Punctuation still marks where a sentence starts and ends.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1725878242"
  quote={`i didn't think you'd understand me
how could you ever even try?`}
/>

The style applies to every letter in the track. The first word of a line stays lowercase, and so does the first-person pronoun.

It also overrides the narrower capitalization rules. A word that would normally be capitalized, such as a name for a deity, follows the style along with everything else:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1694386828"
  quote={"but, god, when i look at you"}
/>

Apply the style to every line or to none of them. A single lowercase line in a normally capitalized song is a transcription mistake, so capitalize it as usual.

## Uppercase throughout

Do not keep a lyric written entirely in uppercase. Capitals give every word the same rectangular outline, so a reader works through the line letter by letter instead of recognizing each word. A synced lyric has to be read in the moment it is on screen, and all capitals make that harder.

Write the words with normal capitalization instead.

<CodeComparison
  correct={`Birds of a feather
We should stick together, I know`}
  incorrect={`BIRDS OF A FEATHER
WE SHOULD STICK TOGETHER, I KNOW`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1739659142" }}
/>

Capitalize the first word of each line, the first-person pronoun, and proper names, and lowercase the rest. Keep capitals that belong to a word itself, such as an acronym or a name that is always written in capitals.

Streaming services carry uppercase lyrics for these releases. Finding one there is not a reason to copy it.

## Stylized titles inside a line

A stylized title can also reach into an otherwise normal lyric. The title mixes capitals in a fixed pattern, and the lines that sing it repeat that pattern.

Keep the pattern on those lines. It belongs to the title the way spelling belongs to a name, and it reaches only the few lines that sing the title in full.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1787023936"
  quote={"DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS de cuando te tuve"}
/>

The same words keep normal capitalization once they are ordinary words again. The next line of this chorus repeats two of them in a new sentence, and both are written as usual.

Do not carry the pattern into words that only echo the title. A stylized title is often an ordinary word underneath, and the lyric uses the ordinary word.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"Tick tock on the clock"}
  incorrect={"TiK ToK on the clock"}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "381237322" }}
/>

Correct the spelling here as well. A title may respell a word to fit its styling, and the lyric keeps the ordinary spelling.
