---
title: Line and syllable splits
description: Where to break lines, and when a word needs syllable timing
---

This page covers both line splits and syllable splits.

A line sync only needs a start and an end for each line. A word sync needs a start and an end for every word, and sometimes for parts of a word. That extra detail is the reason syllable splits exist, and it is also the limit on them. Split a word where you hear it break, and only when there is something to hear.

## Line splits

Line splits decide what the reader sees as one phrase. They apply to every lyric, whether or not it is word-synced.

### Follow the vocal phrase

Start a new line when a melodic or rhythmic phrase ends, a distinct response begins, or the delivery moves into a new thought. Listen to the words in context rather than splitting the text at every breath.

A lyric line can contain a complete sentence, part of a sentence, or a single word. Do not add words to turn a fragment into a sentence, or join clearly separate sung phrases just to complete the grammar.

### Keep connected words together

When the performance allows more than one reasonable break, choose the one that keeps a grammatical phrase together. Avoid separating an article from its noun, an adjective from the word it describes, or the words in a person's name without a musical reason.

When either break fits the delivery:

<CodeComparison
  correctLabel="Prefer"
  incorrectLabel="Avoid"
  correct={`Bittersweet memories
That is all I'm taking with me`}
  incorrect={`Bittersweet memories that
Is all I'm taking with me`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "388151901" }}
/>

Musical phrasing can justify a break within a grammatical phrase. Do not automatically move a word to the next line just because it is an article or preposition.

Here the final words form their own sung phrase:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="388151901"
  quote={`I'll think of you every step of
The way`}
/>

A possessive can end a line the same way when the performance holds it there:

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="us"
  songId="1440659919"
  quote={`I'll be your best friend and you'll be my
Valentine`}
/>

That break looks wrong on the page and is right in the recording. `my` is held at the end of the phrase, and `Valentine` arrives after a pause as its own sung phrase.

Keep the continuation on its own line when that reflects the performance. Capitalize its first word, even though the sentence began earlier.

### Questions across lines

Read the entire question before placing its question mark. Put the mark at the end of the question, not at the end of every line it occupies.

<CodeComparison
  correct={`Do you ever feel like a plastic bag
Drifting through the wind, wanting to start again?`}
  incorrect={`Do you ever feel like a plastic bag?
Drifting through the wind, wanting to start again?`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "716192625" }}
/>

The second line continues the same question. It needs a capital letter, but the first line does not need a question mark or a trailing comma.

Apply the same principle to quotations: keep them open until the quoted passage ends. See [Quotation marks](./punctuation.mdx#quotation-marks).

### Keep line lengths natural

Use the length of the sung phrase to guide the line. Do not force every line to have the same number of words or characters.

- Keep a short hook or response short when it is performed as a separate phrase.
- Split a long passage at a clear musical or grammatical boundary.
- Do not break a word across lyric lines to make it fit.
- Do not insert line breaks solely to match the width of an editor or phone screen. Automatic text wrapping belongs to the display.

Compare repeated passages. Keep their line breaks consistent when the words and delivery are the same, and preserve changes in phrasing when the performance changes.

### Separate the song's sections

Work out where one part of the song ends and the next begins. Verses, choruses, bridges, and other distinct parts are separate sections. A change in tempo or a clear shift from singing to rapping can also mark a section boundary.

A lyrics file has no blank lines. Each part becomes its own section, and the lines inside it follow one after another. See [Song sections](../../../ttml/sections.mdx) for the markup and the labels.

The example marks the boundary with a gap:

<CodeComparison
  correct={`But we are two worlds apart
Can't reach to your heart
When you say
That I want it that way

Tell me why
Ain't nothin' but a heartache`}
  incorrect={`But we are two worlds apart

Can't reach to your heart

When you say

That I want it that way
Tell me why
Ain't nothin' but a heartache`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "283567164" }}
/>

The boundary falls where the verse ends and the chorus begins, not between the lines inside either one. Do not start a new section after every sentence, breath, or change of singer. Do not force every section into four lines. Keep unsung section labels outside the lyric text; the file records the name of a part as an attribute on its section.

### Separate vocal parts

Identify the lead and backing phrases before deciding their line breaks. A backing phrase can overlap the lead or continue after it; it does not have to complete the lead singer's sentence.

For line-synced lyrics, place a short backing phrase in parentheses after the associated lead line. For word-synced lyrics, put it on its own subline and preserve its independent word timing. See [Background vocals](./text.mdx#background-vocals) for formatting examples.

A handoff to another singer may start a new line without starting a new section. Use a section break only when the musical passage also changes.

## Syllable splits

Syllable splits only apply to word-synced lyrics. They divide a word into separately timed parts so the player can follow it while it is being sung.

### A split must earn its place

Most words need no syllable split. One word, one timed interval, is the normal case, and a word sync made only of whole words is a finished word sync.

Split a word only when the split tells the player something a single interval cannot:

- A gap or breath falls inside the word, so the highlight would otherwise run through a silence.
- The performance leans hard on one syllable and the others pass quickly.
- The word is really two words joined together, like `teatime` or `heartbreak`.
- The word is spelled out, chanted, or repeated as a vocalization.

If none of these apply, leave the word whole. A held note is not a reason on its own, and neither is a syllable landing on a new note. Here a word is drawn right out and still needs only one interval, because nothing happens inside it:

<SyllableChips
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1440620921" }}
  ttml={`<p begin="2:36.490" end="2:43.089" itunes:key="L45" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="2:36.490" end="2:36.641">And</span> <span begin="2:36.641" end="2:36.859">I'll</span> <span begin="2:36.859" end="2:37.166">burst</span> <span begin="2:37.166" end="2:37.512">into</span> <span begin="2:37.512" end="2:43.089">flames</span></p>`}
/>

`flames` runs longer than the rest of the line put together and still takes one interval. The note is held, but the word itself never stops and restarts, so there is nothing inside it to mark.

### Split where the sound breaks

When a split is warranted, put the boundary where the next syllable begins in the recording.

The clearest case is an audible gap. The singer holds the first syllable, stops, and returns for the rest of the word:

<SyllableChips
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1537477069" }}
  ttml={`<p begin="11.711" end="16.879" itunes:key="L2" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="11.711" end="12.191">I'm</span> <span begin="12.191" end="12.739">just</span> <span begin="12.739" end="13.317">hoping</span> <span begin="13.317" end="13.589">I</span> <span begin="13.589" end="13.803">don't</span> <span begin="13.803" end="14.327">re</span><span begin="15.508" end="15.905">peat</span> <span begin="15.905" end="16.879">history</span></p>`}
/>

The singer drops out entirely between `re` and `peat`. Without the split, the word would highlight straight through a silence the listener plainly hears. The surrounding words in that same line, `hoping` and `history`, stay whole.

The other everyday case is a word that is really two words joined together. `teatime` is `tea` plus `time`, and the singer treats it that way:

<SyllableChips
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1650841515" }}
  ttml={`<p begin="49.386" end="54.252" itunes:key="L12" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="49.386" end="49.697">At</span> <span begin="49.697" end="50.158">tea</span><span begin="50.887" end="51.508">time,</span> <span begin="52.078" end="53.102">everybody</span> <span begin="53.102" end="54.252">agrees</span></p>`}
/>

The split falls where the two words meet, and the singer leaves a clear break there. `everybody` in the same line is longer still, but it is one word sung straight through, so it takes one interval.

### Follow ordinary syllable boundaries

Split the word the way you hear it. Say it aloud and the break is usually obvious; where it is not, two rules settle it.

A single consonant between vowels starts the next syllable, so it goes with the vowel that follows it.

<CodeComparison
  correctLabel="Prefer"
  incorrectLabel="Avoid"
  correct={`ha | lo
re | peat
to | mor | row`}
  incorrect={`hal | o
rep | eat
tom | or | row`}
/>

A doubled consonant is split down the middle, one letter to each side.

<CodeComparison
  correctLabel="Prefer"
  incorrectLabel="Avoid"
  correct={`fal | ling
lit | tle
shal | low
col | lec | ting`}
  incorrect={`fa | lling
li | ttle
sha | llow
co | llec | ting`}
/>

A common mistake is to break at the note change instead, which lands the `l` on the wrong side:

<SyllableChips
  correctLabel="Prefer"
  incorrectLabel="Avoid"
  correct={`<p begin="1:07.961" end="1:10.810" itunes:key="L15" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="1:07.961" end="1:08.345">Baby,</span> <span begin="1:08.345" end="1:08.545">I</span> <span begin="1:08.545" end="1:08.678">can</span> <span begin="1:08.678" end="1:09.046">see</span> <span begin="1:09.046" end="1:09.228">your</span> <span begin="1:09.228" end="1:09.601">ha</span><span begin="1:09.601" end="1:10.810">lo</span></p>`}
  incorrect={`<p begin="1:07.961" end="1:10.810" itunes:key="L15" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="1:07.961" end="1:08.345">Baby,</span> <span begin="1:08.345" end="1:08.545">I</span> <span begin="1:08.545" end="1:08.678">can</span> <span begin="1:08.678" end="1:09.046">see</span> <span begin="1:09.046" end="1:09.228">your</span> <span begin="1:09.228" end="1:09.601">hal</span><span begin="1:09.601" end="1:10.810">o</span></p>`}
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "296016893" }}
/>

The split itself belongs there, since the word is clearly sung in two parts. Only the letters move.

Moving the boundary does not change the spelling, insert a space or a hyphen, or start a new lyric line. An editor shows each timed part as its own chip, and the parts of one word stay joined:

<SyllableChips
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1740212434" }}
  ttml={`<p begin="21.541" end="23.597" itunes:key="L6" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="21.541" end="21.790">Switch</span> <span begin="21.790" end="21.924">it</span> <span begin="21.924" end="22.178">up</span> <span begin="22.178" end="22.429">like</span> <span begin="22.429" end="22.744">Nin</span><span begin="22.744" end="22.984">ten</span><span begin="22.984" end="23.597">do</span></p>`}
/>

`Nin`, `ten` and `do` carry separate timings while remaining one word. Every other chip is a whole word, and the lyric still reads `Nintendo`.

These two rules describe where a boundary goes, not whether the word needs one. In practice it is easier to split every word into its syllables, sync the whole line against the recording, and then merge back the words whose parts carry no separate timing. Working in that order means the syllable boundaries are already correct wherever a split survives, and [A split must earn its place](#a-split-must-earn-its-place) decides the rest.

### Use the sung pronunciation

Count the syllables the singer performs, not the ones the dictionary lists. Words get compressed and stretched in performance, and what you hear wins.

A syllable carried across several notes is still one syllable. Do not add letters or invent a syllable for each note.

Keep contractions as single written words. An apostrophe is not a syllable boundary. When the performance spells a word out, time the letter names and keep the hyphens between letters and the spaces between words.

### Repeated vocalizations

A repeated sound is written as a repetition and split the same way, because each repeat is sung separately:

<SyllableChips
  source={{ region: "us", songId: "1587414059" }}
  ttml={`<p begin="2:27.337" end="2:29.094" itunes:key="L32" ttm:agent="v1"><span begin="2:27.337" end="2:28.038">Oh-</span><span begin="2:28.038" end="2:28.588">oh-</span><span begin="2:28.588" end="2:29.094">oh</span></p>`}
/>

Each repeat takes its own interval. These are splits between written parts of the lyric, not divisions inside a single word. See [Non-word vocals](./text.mdx#non-word-vocals) for how to write them.

### Check the whole song

Split the same word the same way when it is sung the same way, and let it differ when the delivery differs. A hook sung identically in three choruses should split identically in all three. A final chorus that breaks a word apart may need a split the earlier ones did not.

For encoding lines, words, and syllables in a file, see [Timing in TTML](../../../ttml/timing.mdx).
