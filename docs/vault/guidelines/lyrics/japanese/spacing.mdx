Spaces belong to the displayed lyric, not to its timing grid. A line can contain many timed units without a single written space. [Line and timing splits](./splits.mdx) covers those boundaries.

## Keep Japanese text continuous

Do not put a space between each word, kana, or kanji. Per-word timing uses verified word divisions internally; it does not turn the Japanese lyric into spaced romaji.

For an invented continuous phrase:

<CodeComparison
  correct={"青い空を見て"}
  incorrect={`青い 空 を 見て
青 い 空 を 見 て`}
/>

Switching among kanji, hiragana, and katakana does not require a space. Keep kanji and their following kana together in the displayed text, even when those characters have separate timestamps.

That space separates two phrases. The timing boundaries inside each phrase leave no mark on the written line.

## Phrase spaces

A space can separate two phrases that still belong on one lyric line. Use it at a clear phrase boundary, not at every breath or grammatical junction. Keep deliberate phrase spacing from a reliable lyric source when it fits the performance.

An invented line with two short phrases might read:

```text
目を閉じて 耳を澄まして
```

A released lyric uses the same kind of selective phrase space:

<AppleMusicQuote region="jp" songId="1484037111" quote={"きみみたいに きれいな女の子が"} />

The space groups the text for reading. It does not assert that the singer is silent there. A larger change of phrase may need a [new line](./splits.mdx#line-splits) instead.

Do not impose a fixed number of spaces on each line or import the Chinese guide's phrase layout into Japanese. One continuous phrase needs no space. Where the performance allows several positions, keep a word, a name, or a closely connected grammatical group whole.

## Use one plain space

Use a single ordinary space, U+0020, where a space is needed. Remove leading and trailing spaces. Do not use tabs or several spaces to line up the text or represent a longer rest.

Check copied text for these characters:

| Character | Name | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `U+0020` | Ordinary space | Use for a phrase or word space |
| `U+3000` | Ideographic space | Replace with one ordinary space |
| `U+00A0` | No-break space | Replace with one ordinary space |
| `U+200B` | Zero-width space | Remove if introduced by copying |

An ideographic space is a real Japanese typographic space, but its extra width is not needed in the delivered lyric. Preserve the phrase boundary when replacing it. Do not delete all whitespace indiscriminately: English words still need their spaces.

## Mixed scripts

Put one ordinary space on each side of a Latin run where it meets Japanese text. Capitalize the run's first word, then follow normal English capitalization within it.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"Okay たちまち独壇場 Listen listen"}
  incorrect={"Okayたちまち独壇場Listen listen"}
  source={{ region: "jp", songId: "1704093812" }}
/>

Apple Music omits both script-boundary spaces in the archived line. Adding them makes the Latin runs visible without creating lyric lines or timing boundaries.

The same rule applies when a Latin unit symbol touches Japanese:

<CodeComparison
  correct={"半径300mm の体で 必死に鳴いてる"}
  incorrect={"半径300mmの体で 必死に鳴いてる"}
  source={{ region: "jp", songId: "1657318765" }}
/>

Digits do not need spaces against Japanese, so `半径300` stays together. The space goes after the Latin run `mm`, where Japanese resumes. Keep a measurement symbol's standard case rather than changing `mm` to `Mm`.

Drop a boundary space only at the start or end of a line. Preserve acronyms and proper names, and capitalize ordinary English according to the [English text rules](../english/text.mdx#capitalization). A script-boundary space remains part of the displayed text even when the timing spans divide somewhere else.

## Around punctuation

Keep Japanese punctuation tight against the text it belongs to. Do not put spaces immediately inside quotation brackets or parentheses.

<CodeComparison correct={"「おかえり」と言う"} incorrect={"「 おかえり 」 と言う"} />

A line-synced backing response has one space before its opening parenthesis; see [Background vocals](./text.mdx#background-vocals). A reading annotation raises no spacing question, because it does not stay in the lyric; see [Reading annotations](./text.mdx#reading-annotations).

Inside an English passage, follow [English punctuation spacing](../english/spacing.mdx). Do not add spaces around a long-vowel mark, an iteration mark, or an apostrophe within a word.
