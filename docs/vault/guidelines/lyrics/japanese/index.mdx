---
title: Japanese
description: Guidelines for Japanese lyrics
---

Write lyrics that match the recording and are easy to follow. Preserve the artist's wording and written forms, and let the vocal phrasing guide the line breaks.

## Keep the artist's words

Keep intentional kanji, kana, contractions, dialect, and mixed-language passages. Do not rewrite the lyric in kana to make it easier to time, or replace an unusual expression with formal Japanese.

Use the exact version of the recording you are transcribing. Check unclear wording against an artist- or publisher-provided lyric source. The audio establishes what is performed; a reliable written source helps distinguish spellings that sound alike.

## Choose a timing approach

- **Per word:** time each word as a unit, using the word divisions of a verified romanization as a guide. Do not combine unrelated words into a single phrase-sized span.
- **Per character, recommended:** use smaller, reading-aware units of the original text. Keep characters together where separating them would invent a pronunciation boundary. This gives finer highlighting without rewriting the lyric in kana.

More boundaries give finer control, not automatic accuracy. Both approaches need timing checked against the recording. The [splitting guide](./splits.mdx) explains the exceptions to literal character splitting.

## Use sources critically

Apple Music TTML is useful evidence of wording and presentation, but its word-timed files mix character-sized, word-sized, and phrase-sized spans. The `Word` timing label does not guarantee linguistic word boundaries. Imported romanization also needs checking.

Keep these decisions separate: what is written, how it is sung, where the line breaks, and which parts receive individual timestamps. Do not change spelling or add spaces just to expose timing boundaries.

## Make the text readable

- Keep continuous Japanese text continuous, including across timed spans.
- Use punctuation for meaning, not as a record of every breath or note.
- Break lines at vocal phrases rather than a fixed character count.
- Write each repeated passage in full and check its wording and delivery again.
- Keep reading annotations out of the lyric text, and keep lead and backing vocals distinct.
- Put verses, choruses, and other musical sections in their own sections in the file.
