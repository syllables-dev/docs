---
title: Background vocals
description: Represent backing vocals as independently timed sublines
---

A backing subline belongs to a lead lyric line but has its own text and timing. It can begin before the lead, overlap it, or continue after it.

For word-synced lyrics, represent that subline with a grouping `<span>` inside the lead line's `<p>`. Put `ttm:role="x-bg"` on the grouping span and give its words their own timed spans.

## Mark the backing subline

This fragment belongs inside a TTML document that declares the `ttm` metadata namespace, as shown in [Understanding TTML](./index.mdx#read-a-lyrics-document):

```xml
<p begin="00:10.000" end="00:13.000">
  <span begin="00:10.000" end="00:10.300">Will</span>
  <span begin="00:10.300" end="00:10.600">you</span>
  <span begin="00:10.600" end="00:11.200">stay?</span>
  <span ttm:role="x-bg">
    <span begin="00:10.900" end="00:11.600">Stay</span>
    <span begin="00:11.600" end="00:12.100">with</span>
    <span begin="00:12.100" end="00:13.000">me</span>
  </span>
</p>
```

The lead ends at 11.2 seconds. The backing subline starts at 10.9 seconds and ends at thirteen seconds. The parent line includes both intervals, so it ends at thirteen seconds.

The role applies to the entire backing phrase. Do not put it on the parent `<p>`, which also contains the lead, or treat each backing word as a separate subline.

Use an [agent reference](./agents.mdx) to identify who performs the words. The backing role does not automatically make that performer an `other` or `group` agent.

## Keep the vocal parts separate

- Keep the backing words inside their grouping span rather than interleaving them with the lead words.
- Preserve each part's timing, including overlaps.
- Present the backing phrase as its own subline rather than appending it to the lead text in parentheses.
- Keep an interjection performed by the lead vocalist in the lead part. Short words and vocal sounds are not automatically backing vocals.

The role identifies the vocal part. Parentheses alone do not create a backing subline or give it independent timing. Apple's delivery examples include parentheses inside some marked backing spans; the grouping and role are what identify the backing part.

## Order the subline within its parent

Apple's lyrics profile places the background grouping span either first or last inside the parent line:

- Put it first when the backing vocal starts before the lead.
- Otherwise, put it after the lead spans.

Document order keeps the structure readable. Timestamps determine when each part is performed, so placing the backing span last does not mean it starts after the lead has finished.

## Line-synced lyrics

A line-synced lyric has one interval for the whole line. Use the [line-synced background-vocal formatting](../lyrics-guidelines/english/text.mdx#line-synced-lyrics) for the text. When creating a word-synced version, identify the backing phrase, give it a subline, and time its words independently.
