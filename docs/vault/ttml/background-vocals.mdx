---
title: Background vocals
description: Represent backing vocals as independently timed sublines
---

A backing subline belongs to a lead lyric line but has its own text and timing. It can begin before the lead, overlap it, or continue after it.

For word-synced lyrics, represent that subline with a grouping `<span>` inside the lead line's `<p>`. Put `ttm:role="x-bg"` on the grouping span and give its words their own timed spans.

## Mark the backing subline

This fragment belongs inside a TTML document that declares the `ttm` metadata namespace, as shown in [Understanding TTML](./index.mdx#read-a-lyrics-document):

```xml
<p begin="00:10.000" end="00:13.000" ttm:agent="v1">
  <span begin="00:10.000" end="00:10.300">Will </span>
  <span begin="00:10.300" end="00:10.600">you </span>
  <span begin="00:10.600" end="00:11.200">stay?</span>
  <span ttm:role="x-bg" ttm:agent="v2">
    <span begin="00:10.900" end="00:11.600">Stay </span>
    <span begin="00:11.600" end="00:12.100">with </span>
    <span begin="00:12.100" end="00:13.000">me</span>
  </span>
</p>
```

The parent line covers both parts, so its interval runs to the end of whichever finishes last.

The role applies to the entire backing phrase. Do not put it on the parent `<p>`, which also contains the lead, and do not treat each backing word as a separate subline. Keep one backing group per line; the player ignores any further groups on the same line.

## Order the subline within its parent

Put the grouping span first inside the `<p>` when the backing vocal starts before the lead. Otherwise put it after the lead spans.

Document order keeps the structure readable. Timestamps determine when each part is performed, so a backing span placed last does not mean it starts after the lead has finished.

## Name the performer

Use an [agent reference](./agents.mdx) to identify who performs the words. The backing role does not automatically make that performer an `other` or `group` agent.

A backing part can have its own agent. Put `ttm:agent` on the grouping span when the backing is sung by someone other than the line's performer, as in the example above. Leave it off when the same performer sings both parts.

## Keep the vocal parts separate

- Keep the backing words inside their grouping span rather than interleaving them with the lead words.
- Preserve each part's timing, including overlaps.
- Present the backing phrase as its own subline rather than appending it to the lead text.
- Keep an interjection performed by the lead vocalist in the lead part. Short words and vocal sounds are not automatically backing vocals.

The role identifies the vocal part. Parentheses do not create a backing subline or give it independent timing. Apple's files often wrap backing text in parentheses, and the player removes that wrapping pair unless the file asks to keep it.

## Line-synced lyrics

A line-synced lyric has one interval for the whole line. Use the [line-synced background-vocal formatting](../lyrics-guidelines/lyrics/english/text.mdx#line-synced-lyrics) for the text. When creating a word-synced version, identify the backing phrase, give it a subline, and time its words independently.
