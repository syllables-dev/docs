title: Chinese
description: Guidelines for Chinese lyrics
---

Write the characters as they are sung, separate sung phrases with a space, and leave the punctuation out. These guidelines cover Mandarin and Cantonese together, because both follow the same writing conventions. The few differences are called out where they apply.

## Submit traditional characters

Submit every Chinese lyric in traditional characters, Mandarin and Cantonese alike, whatever script the release uses. The server derives the simplified version from your submission.

Conversion only runs safely in that direction. One simplified character often stands for several traditional ones, so going the other way means guessing at the word, and a wrong guess changes the lyric. See [Submit traditional characters](./text.mdx#submit-traditional-characters).

## Keep the artist's words

Script is the one thing you normalize. Preserve the wording and the register of the released lyric: do not rewrite colloquial Cantonese into standard written Chinese, and do not modernize older vocabulary.

Use the exact version of the recording you are transcribing. A live performance, a Mandarin version of a Cantonese song, or a clean edit may have different words.

Apple Music transcriptions can contain mistakes. Trust your ears, and cross-reference different sources whenever possible.

## Make the text readable

- Keep one set of variant characters throughout the song.
- Leave out punctuation. A space, not a comma, separates phrases inside a line.
- Write repeated characters and repeated lines in full each time they are performed.
- Break lines at natural vocal phrases. A sentence may continue across several lines.
- Put each verse, chorus, and other distinct part of the song in its own section.
- A Chinese character is one syllable, so it is never split into parts.
