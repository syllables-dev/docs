Transcribe the characters as performed. This page covers which characters go in the file; [Spacing](./spacing.mdx) and [Punctuation](./punctuation.mdx) cover how the line is marked up.

This page applies to Mandarin and Cantonese alike. Where the two differ, the difference is in which characters the lyric uses, not in how the line is written.

## Submit traditional characters

Submit every Chinese lyric in traditional characters, Mandarin and Cantonese alike, whatever script the release uses. The server derives the simplified version from what you submit, so a reader who wants simplified still gets it. Submitting simplified gives the server nothing to work from.

Traditional is the submission script because conversion only runs safely in that direction. Traditional to simplified is largely many-to-one and needs no judgement. Simplified to traditional is one-to-many, so a converter has to guess which word was meant:

```text
发 -> 髮 or 發
干 -> 乾, 幹, or 干
面 -> 麵 or 面
后 -> 後 or 后
里 -> 裡 or 里
```

A wrong guess is a wrong word in the lyric. Choosing between them takes the sense of the line, which is why the choice belongs to you rather than to the pipeline.

Use traditional from beginning to end. A song does not switch script partway through, and a verse transcribed from a different source does not get to keep that source's script.

<CodeComparison
  correctLabel="Prefer"
  incorrectLabel="Avoid"
  correct={`從不結果 無論種什麼夢
慶幸的是我 一直沒回頭`}
  incorrect={`从不结果 无论种什么梦
慶幸的是我 一直沒回頭`}
  source={{ region: "tw", songId: "820292356" }}
/>

When the release is simplified, convert it yourself and read every line as you go. Do not paste the output of an automatic converter without checking it, because that is the guess this rule exists to avoid.

## Keep the artist's words

Script is the one thing you normalize. Follow the released lyric in everything else: its wording, its register, and the variant characters it chooses. The words the artist approved are the words that belong in the file.

### Keep variant characters consistent

Several characters have two accepted traditional forms. Both are correct, so pick the one the lyric sheet uses and keep it for the whole song.

```text
裡 / 裏
著 / 着
什 / 甚
麼 / 麽
為 / 爲
```

Do not normalize one line to a form the rest of the song does not use, and do not let a second form appear because a later verse was transcribed from a different source.

A simplified release often leaves no variant choice to inherit, because several of these pairs collapse to a single simplified character. Pick one form when you convert, and use it everywhere the character appears.

### Write Cantonese as the lyric writes it

Most Cantopop is written in standard written Chinese, which reads as Mandarin on the page and is sung in Cantonese. A smaller number of songs are written in spoken Cantonese, using characters like `唔`, `係`, `嘅`, `咁`, `乜嘢`, and `冇`.

Both are correct. Write whichever one the song uses, and do not convert between them.

Spoken Cantonese, kept as written:

Do not rewrite `係` as `是` because the second looks more formal, and do not rewrite `是` as `係` because the track is Cantonese. The romanization differs between the two, so changing the characters changes what the file says was sung. See [Cantonese romanization](./romanization/cantonese.mdx).

### Follow the lyric's choice of pronoun

Chinese distinguishes written pronouns that sound identical. `你` and `妳` are both *nǐ*, and `他`, `她`, and `牠` are all *tā*. The choice carries meaning, so follow the lyric sheet rather than defaulting to the common form.

<AppleMusicQuote region="tw" songId="160857031" quote={"妳還在想著他嗎"} />

That line uses `妳` for the woman being addressed and `他` for the man she is thinking of. Collapsing both to the default forms would lose the distinction the writer made.

Keep the choice consistent within a song unless the lyric deliberately switches.

## Repeated characters

Write every repeat in full. Do not collapse a repeated character into a count, and do not join repeats with a hyphen or an enumeration comma. Repetition of a whole character is written as repetition of the character.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"我我我我也也 已經是個糟老頭"}
  incorrect={`我、我、我、我、也、也 已經是個糟老頭
我-我-我-我也-也 已經是個糟老頭`}
  source={{ region: "tw", songId: "657693184" }}
/>

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="tw"
  songId="657693184"
  quote={"時光匆匆匆匆流走 也也也不回頭"}
/>

That second line repeats `匆匆` as a pair and `也` three times on its own. Both are performed and both are written out. For how each repeat is split, see [Repeated characters](./splits.mdx#repeated-characters).

## Background vocals

### Line-synced lyrics

For line-based synchronization only, put a short backing phrase in half-width parentheses at the end of the associated lead line, following the [Apple Music for Artists convention](https://artists.apple.com/support/1111-lyrics-guidelines). Use no space inside the parentheses, and separate them from the lead text with one space.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="tw"
  songId="1078561855"
  quote={"一波還未平息 (一波還未平息 一波又來侵襲)"}
/>

### Word-synced lyrics

Put the backing phrase on its own subline beneath the associated lead line. The subline keeps its own words, its own internal spaces, and its own parentheses, and it stays independent of the lead even where the two vocals overlap.

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="tw"
  songId="1078561855"
  quote={`一波還未平息
(一波還未平息 一波又來侵襲)`}
/>

See [Background vocals in TTML](../../ttml/background-vocals.mdx) for the file structure of a backing subline.

An interjection sung by the lead vocalist stays in the lead text. A delay tail or a reverb wash is the production repeating the lead rather than a second performer, so it gets no subline.

## Non-word vocals

Include recognizable non-word vocals when they form part of the melody, hook, or a meaningful response. Write them with the characters that spell that sound in Chinese.

```text
啦   哦   喔   噢
嗚   啊   呀   哎
哈   呵   哼   嘿
```

<AppleMusicQuote
  region="tw"
  songId="657693184"
  quote={"啦啦啦啦 啦啦啦啦 啦啦啦啦啦啦"}
/>

Keep a vocalization in Latin script when that is how the song performs it. `Oh`, `Ooh`, `Whoa`, `Ah`, and `La-la-la` are ordinary in Mandarin and Cantonese recordings, and they follow the [English spellings](../english/text.mdx#non-word-vocals), including the hyphens that join connected sounds and the commas that separate them.

Do not add extra characters to represent a held note, and do not transcribe every incidental breath. Leave out vocals that production has turned into texture rather than words.

## Spoken passages

Transcribe audible spoken passages and conversational interludes. Spoken Cantonese appears in spoken passages far more often than in sung ones, so a track written in standard written Chinese may still carry `唔`, `係`, and `嘅` in its dialogue. Write what is said.

Keep speaker labels, section headings, and editorial instructions outside the lyric text. See [Agents and voices](../../ttml/agents.mdx#spoken-passages) for speaker assignments in the file.

## Clean and explicit lyrics

Match the version of the recording. Transcribe uncensored words in full when they are audible, and censor a word only when the audio censors it. When the recording bleeps or mutes part or all of a word, replace the whole word with asterisks rather than leaving part of it visible.

If the singer performs a replacement word in a clean version, transcribe that word. Do not restore words from the explicit version or add censorship that is absent from the recording.

## Check for look-alike characters

Some characters have a visually identical twin elsewhere in Unicode, and Kangxi radicals are the common case. `⼼` (U+2F3C) and `心` (U+5FC3) render the same way in most fonts, but only the second is the character. The look-alike survives a copy and paste out of a PDF or an image caption, renders correctly, and breaks search, sorting, romanization, and text-to-speech.

<CodeComparison
  correct={"我發覺心裡有條裂痕"}
  incorrect={"我發覺⼼裡有條裂痕"}
  source={{ region: "hk", songId: "1879436477" }}
/>

Those two lines differ only in the third character.

Type Chinese text with a standard input method rather than copying glyphs out of an image caption, and check the file before delivery. Spaces have the same problem, covered in [Use a plain space, not a look-alike](./spacing.mdx#use-a-plain-space-not-a-look-alike).
